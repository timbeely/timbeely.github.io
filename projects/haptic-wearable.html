Project Overview

During my Summer 2026 electrical engineering internship, I worked on the development of a battery-powered wearable haptic feedback system intended to provide configurable tactile cues during athletic training. The project combined embedded hardware, firmware, wireless communication, haptic feedback, and mobile-app integration in a compact wearable system.

My contributions included custom PCB design, embedded C++ firmware, Bluetooth Low Energy communication, haptic-driver integration, rechargeable-battery power management, and mobile-app connectivity. I also supported system testing and iterative debugging across the hardware and software stack.

The final system demonstrated reliable wireless configuration, configurable tactile feedback, and battery-powered operation in a wearable form factor.

System Architecture

This project was developed for industry, so proprietary schematics, PCB schematics and layout details, product imagery, source code, and internal documentation are not publicly displayed.

At a high level, the system connected a mobile application to an embedded wearable over Bluetooth Low Energy. The wearable used an ESP32-C3 to coordinate wireless communication, haptic feedback, timing, and power-management behavior.

Technical Stack

  • Microcontroller: ESP32-C3
  • Firmware: Embedded C++
  • Wireless Communication: Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)
  • Peripheral Communication: I2C
  • Haptic Feedback: LRA-based haptic system
  • PCB Design: KiCad
  • Power: Rechargeable lithium-battery system
  • Mobile Integration: React Native, Expo, TypeScript, and iOS development tools (Xcode, CocoaPods)

Engineering Approach

Hardware and Power Design

I worked on the embedded hardware required to integrate the microcontroller, haptic subsystem, power system, and supporting interfaces into a wearable platform. The design needed to balance functionality, compactness, and battery-powered operation.

Power consumption was an important system constraint, so both hardware and firmware considerations were used to support practical wearable runtime without sacrificing responsive system behavior.

Firmware and Haptic Control

I developed embedded C++ firmware to coordinate wireless communication, timing, tactile feedback, and device-state management. The firmware was designed to support responsive operation while maintaining reliable communication with the companion mobile application.

Multiple configurable haptic patterns were implemented so the system could provide different tactile cues depending on the active training configuration.

BLE and Mobile Integration

Bluetooth Low Energy was used to connect the wearable to a companion mobile application. The application allowed training settings and feedback behavior to be configured wirelessly without physically interacting with the embedded device.

I worked on the communication between the mobile and embedded sides of the system and supported reliable configuration updates during normal operation.

Testing and Validation

Development involved repeated hardware and firmware testing to verify wireless communication, tactile feedback behavior, power operation, and overall system reliability.

The completed wearable achieved more than four hours of active battery runtime and demonstrated consistent haptic feedback and reliable wireless configuration during testing.

Key Takeaways

  • Gained experience developing a complete embedded system spanning hardware, firmware, wireless communication, haptic actuation, and mobile integration.
  • Strengthened my embedded C++ development and debugging skills while working with a real battery-powered device.
  • Learned how hardware, firmware, wireless communication, and power management must be designed and tested together at the system level.
  • Gained experience iterating on a product intended for real-world use rather than a purely academic prototype.
