Project Overview

During my Summer 2026 electrical engineering internship, I worked on the development of a battery-powered wearable haptic feedback system designed to provide configurable tactile cues during athletic training. The device combined embedded hardware, firmware, Bluetooth Low Energy communication, haptic actuation, and mobile-app control in a compact wearable platform.

My work included custom PCB design, ESP32-C3 firmware development, integration of a dedicated LRA haptic driver, BLE communication, rechargeable-battery power management, and mobile-app integration. The firmware coordinated wireless configuration, tactile-pattern playback, actuator calibration, connection management, and low-power operation.

The final system supported configurable training intervals and multiple tactile feedback patterns while maintaining reliable wireless control and battery-powered operation.

Engineering Approach

Hardware and Power Design

The wearable was built around the ESP32-C3 to provide embedded processing and Bluetooth Low Energy communication in a small form factor. I worked on the custom PCB design used to integrate the microcontroller, haptic subsystem, power electronics, and supporting interfaces into the wearable platform.

Because the system was intended for portable athletic use, power consumption and battery life were important design constraints. Rechargeable-battery protection and power-management functionality were incorporated into the hardware, while firmware-level low-power behavior was used to reduce unnecessary current draw when the device was not actively operating.

Firmware and Haptic Control

I developed embedded C++ firmware to coordinate the major functions of the wearable. The runtime architecture handled BLE communication, interval timing, haptic playback, calibration state, connection management, and power-state transitions.

Timing for recurring tactile cues was implemented using a non-blocking approach based on system time rather than long blocking delays. This allowed the main loop to continue processing wireless communication, device-state changes, and power-management logic while maintaining configurable feedback intervals.

Multiple tactile patterns were implemented so the mobile application could select different physical cues depending on the training configuration. Haptic playback was controlled through a dedicated driver over I2C, with firmware managing waveform selection and playback sequencing.

Haptic Calibration and Persistent Configuration

The haptic subsystem used a linear resonant actuator (LRA), requiring appropriate driver configuration to achieve consistent tactile feedback. I implemented an automatic calibration routine that characterized the actuator and stored successful calibration parameters in the ESP32's non-volatile memory.

Once calibration had been attempted, the firmware could restore saved parameters on later power cycles rather than repeatedly running the higher-current calibration process. The system also included failure handling and fallback drive parameters so the wearable could continue operating if automatic calibration did not complete successfully.

A wireless recalibration command was also implemented. Recalibration requests were deferred to the main firmware loop rather than executed directly inside the BLE callback, reducing the risk of interfering with the Bluetooth stack while reinitializing the haptic configuration.

BLE and Mobile Integration

Bluetooth Low Energy was used to configure the wearable from a mobile application. The firmware exposed a BLE service that received configuration data containing training timing and tactile-pattern selections.

Incoming commands were parsed on the ESP32-C3 and used to update the active training configuration without requiring a firmware restart. The device also monitored connection state and automatically resumed BLE advertising after a disconnect, allowing the wearable to reconnect to a mobile device without a manual reset.

The companion application provided the higher-level configuration interface used to control wearable behavior and manage training settings wirelessly.

Power Management

To reduce standby power consumption, I implemented a deep-sleep state tied to the wearable's physical power control. Before entering deep sleep, the firmware stopped active haptic playback and placed the haptic driver into standby.

The ESP32-C3 then configured a GPIO-based wake condition and entered deep sleep, allowing the device to remain in a very low-power state until the physical control returned the system to its active state. On wake, the firmware restored the required peripherals, BLE advertising, and haptic configuration.

Testing and Validation

Development involved iterative hardware and firmware testing across the complete signal path, including BLE communication, haptic-driver control, actuator response, calibration behavior, reconnection logic, power-state transitions, and repeated interval playback.

The completed wearable achieved more than four hours of active runtime and supported strong LRA-based tactile feedback, including actuator operation near its intended resonant frequency. Testing also verified reliable configuration updates, repeated haptic triggering, automatic BLE recovery after disconnection, and proper transition between active and low-power states.

Key Takeaways

  • Gained experience developing a complete embedded system spanning custom hardware, firmware, wireless communication, actuation, and mobile integration.
  • Applied non-blocking firmware design to coordinate timing, BLE communication, tactile feedback, and device-state management within a single embedded application.
  • Implemented persistent actuator calibration and failure handling to improve repeatability across power cycles.
  • Designed low-power behavior using peripheral standby, ESP32 deep sleep, and GPIO-based wake control for battery-powered operation.
  • Strengthened my understanding of debugging systems where hardware, firmware, power electronics, wireless communication, and mechanical actuation must operate together reliably.
